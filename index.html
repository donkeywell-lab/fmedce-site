Medical intelligence

The monthly briefing

One therapeutic area per subscription. Five parts, delivered as a PDF each month.

Landscape monitoring usually arrives as either a raw literature dump nobody reads or a vendor deck written by someone who has never sat across from an investigator. This is the middle: a structured monthly read of everything that moved, filtered to what a field medical or clinical development team would act on.

  1. 01

    Executive summary

    What changed this month, in the order it matters.

  2. 02

    Positive topline results and impact

    Readouts that shift standard of care, competitive positioning, or the questions your teams will be asked.

  3. 03

    Systematic evidence mapping

    New literature mapped by segment, ranked by journal impact and recency.

  4. 04

    Clinical trial landscape deltas

    Not a list of trials — the specific registry changes: new starts, status shifts, enrollment and endpoint edits.

  5. 05

    Digital voice and expert sentiment

    Where the scientific conversation is heading, from expert opinion, congress consensus, and clinician social channels.

Evidence sweep

A full PubMed pass over the prior 45 days, reduced to the publications that matter.

Registry deltas

ClinicalTrials.gov compared across 30-, 60- and 90-day windows, so movement is visible rather than inferred.

Sourced and checkable

Every trial and publication carries its identifier. Nothing here is a claim you cannot trace yourself.

Training and development

Capability that survives the workshop

Scoped directly with me. No packages, no talent-network funnel.

Most field medical training is a two-day event that leaves no trace by the next cycle meeting. These programmes are built from the fascicles — a written model the team can re-read, a workshop that practises it, field work that applies it, and an assessment a manager can actually score.

  • Scientific exchange and insight gathering

    The five-pillar engagement model from Fascicle No. 02, taught as a workshop and reinforced in the field. Ends with insight quality your medical strategy team can use, not meeting counts.

    • Engagement design
    • Question architecture
    • Insight capture
    • Field reinforcement

  • Core and effortless competencies

    The ten competencies from Fascicles No. 03 and No. 04, converted into a proficiency framework with observable behaviours at each level — so "clinical acumen" stops being a word on a form.

    • Proficiency rubric
    • Manager calibration
    • Development plans
    • Re-assessment

  • New teams and new managers

    Team formation, first-line leadership transition, and hiring design — including the interview structure that separates a good scientist from a good MSL.

    • Team charter
    • Manager transition
    • Interview design
    • Onboarding curriculum

Also available: advisory board planning and execution, end to end — objectives, expert selection, discussion design, and the insight synthesis that makes the meeting worth its cost.
